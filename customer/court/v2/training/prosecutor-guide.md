# Prosecutor guide

> **Not yet approved for demos.** This page describes the current Court V2 build, which is newer than the version approved for demos. Use it for training and planning. Check [Demoable now](../demoable-now.md) before showing these screens to a customer.

**Who this is for:** staff with the court prosecutor role, such as the city attorney or district attorney for the court.

The prosecutor role is narrow. It can read cases, sign and file motions to dismiss under CCP 32.02, and object to Youth Diversion under CCP 45A.504(d). It can't change a case's state, money or pleas. A prosecutor never signs an order of dismissal and never rules on a request.

This guide covers only those two jobs. Other prosecutor work happens outside Thin Line.

## 1. Open the Prosecutor Queue

1. Select **Prosecutor Queue** in the Court Violations menu, or switch the **Dashboard** to **Prosecutor** mode.
2. If you work for more than one court, pick the court agency first. Until you do, the page says: *Select a court agency to see the Prosecutor Queue.*
3. The queue has four lists:

| List | What's in it |
|------|--------------|
| **Motions to dismiss awaiting prosecutor review** | Dismissal requests the clerk sent you, and your own motions the judge returned for correction |
| **Motions to dismiss awaiting the judge** | Motions you signed that the judge hasn't ruled on yet |
| **Motions to dismiss denied** | Motions the judge denied in the last 30 days |
| **Youth diversion requests open for objection** | Youth Diversion requests you can still object to |

## 2. Approve and sign a clerk's request

When the clerk sends a **Prosecutor motion to dismiss (CCP 32.02)** request, it waits in the first list.

1. On the card, select **Review case and documents** to read the case and the State's reasons the clerk entered.
2. Select **Approve and sign**.
3. Review the reasons, draw your signature, and select **Approve and sign** again.
4. The signed motion goes to the Judge Queue and moves to **Motions to dismiss awaiting the judge**.

The case is dismissed only when the judge signs the order of dismissal. Your signature is on the motion, not the order.

## 3. Decline a clerk's request

1. On the card, select **Decline**.
2. Enter a note. A note is required.
3. The request is closed. The clerk sees it in **Clerk Queue > Fee / follow-up** as **Prosecutor declined dismissal request**.

## 4. File your own motion from a case

You can file a motion to dismiss without a clerk request.

1. Open the case. It must be in Pre-plea, Post-plea pre-judgment, Pre-trial, Trial or Verdict returned, with no other open request.
2. Select **Sign and file motion**.
3. Enter the reasons, draw your signature, and file.
4. The motion goes to the Judge Queue.

If the case already has an open request, wait until the judge decides it, or until it's revoked or cancelled.

## 5. Correct a motion the judge returned

When the judge selects **Return for correction**, the motion comes back to **Motions to dismiss awaiting prosecutor review**.

1. Open the motion and correct it.
2. Select **Sign and resubmit to judge**.

## 6. What the judge can do with your motion

| Judge's choice | Result |
|----------------|--------|
| **Sign order and dismiss** | The case is dismissed and closed. |
| **Return for correction** | The motion comes back to you. See section 5. |
| **Deny motion** | The motion is denied and listed under **Motions to dismiss denied** for 30 days. The clerk follows up. |

## 7. Object to Youth Diversion

Youth Diversion isn't granted when the prosecutor objects (CCP 45A.504(d)).

1. In **Youth diversion requests open for objection**, open the request.
2. Select **Object to youth diversion**.
3. Enter a note of up to 2,000 characters and submit.

You'll see a message instead if the case belongs to a court you aren't the prosecutor for, if Youth Diversion has already been decided, or if an objection is already on file.

If you object on paper, the clerk records your paper objection on the request.

## 8. Things to know

- A dismissal request where the clerk recorded a paper motion you already signed goes straight to the judge. It doesn't appear in any of your lists.
- You never sign the order of dismissal. On a dismissal card the judge sees: *A court attorney cannot sign an order of dismissal. The judge signs it.*
- You can read cases but can't run outcomes, take payments, enter pleas or change case dates.
- You can't record the Youth Diversion decision. A clerk or the judge records it.

## Practice checklist

- [ ] Pick a court agency and open the Prosecutor Queue.
- [ ] Approve and sign a clerk's motion request.
- [ ] Decline a request with a note.
- [ ] File your own motion from a case.
- [ ] Resubmit a motion the judge returned.
- [ ] Object to a Youth Diversion request.
